What our DLF Phase 5 car inspection covers

The inspection is designed around the buyer's decision and covers major observable vehicle areas. Exact checks vary by vehicle, package, access and whether a system can be safely operated or tested at the inspection location.

Exterior, Paint & Body

Body panels, paint finish, panel gaps, dents, scratches, glass, mirrors, lights and visible repair indicators are reviewed. Paint-thickness measurements may be used where appropriate.

Tyres, Wheels & Brakes

Tyre condition, visible damage, tread-related observations, wheel condition and accessible brake-related observations are checked and recorded.

Interior & Electrical

Dashboard warning indicators, controls, infotainment, AC, windows, locks, seats, cameras where testable and other accessible electrical features are reviewed.

Engine Bay & Mechanical Areas

Accessible engine-bay observations and visible mechanical-condition indicators are reviewed. Road testing or component-level diagnosis is only performed when safe and included in the selected inspection.

VIN, Manufacturing & Delivery Checks

VIN-related details, visible manufacturing information, tyre information and delivery items are checked to identify mismatches or points requiring clarification before handover.

OBD Diagnostics

OBD scanning is available on selected packages and compatible vehicles to read available diagnostic information. A scan cannot identify every fault or independently verify actual mileage.

Warning Lights & Controls

Visible warning indicators and accessible vehicle controls are checked so the report can distinguish normal operation, observed issues and items requiring further investigation.

360° Condition Review

A structured walk-around documents the vehicle from multiple sides and reduces the chance that visible condition items are overlooked during a rushed handover or purchase inspection.

Inspection Report & Evidence

Findings are organised into a customer-facing report with observations and evidence, helping you decide what should be clarified, repaired, negotiated or checked further.

BUYER DECISION SUPPORT

When should you book a car inspection in DLF Phase 5?

Before new-car delivery

Ideally, book the PDI before final handover. This gives you time to document visible paint and body condition, glass, lights, tyres, interior features, VIN-related details, warning indicators and delivery items before acceptance.

Before buying a used or old car

Arrange the inspection before payment or final commitment whenever possible. An inspection can highlight visible repair indicators, cosmetic issues, tyre condition, electrical concerns and other observations that deserve clarification or negotiation.

Before buying a premium or luxury vehicle

Premium vehicles can have higher repair and replacement costs. A condition-focused inspection helps you make the decision using documented observations rather than appearance, age or seller claims alone.

Before buying an EV

An EV inspection can review accessible vehicle condition and available diagnostic information, but battery health or hidden electronic faults should not be assumed from a basic visual inspection. The report can identify where specialist testing is required.

Before a residential, office or approved vehicle-site handover

If the vehicle is being delivered to a home, office, commercial premises or approved vehicle site, the inspection can be arranged around the handover timing after access and safety conditions are confirmed.

INDEPENDENT BUYER PROTECTION

Why an independent inspection helps DLF Phase 5 car buyers

A vehicle handover or used-car purchase can move quickly, making it easy to overlook small condition issues. An independent inspection adds a structured second look before you accept delivery or commit to a purchase.

For a new car, the inspection complements the seller's delivery process rather than replacing it. The objective is to create an independent record of observable condition across paint, body, tyres, glass, lights, interior features, VIN-related details, warning indicators and delivery items.

For a used or old car, the inspection is most useful before payment. Findings can help you separate normal age-related wear from visible issues that deserve clarification, negotiation or specialist diagnosis.

For premium vehicles and EVs, we report what can be safely observed or tested and clearly identify limitations. We do not claim that a visual inspection alone can prove battery health, hidden electronic faults or every mechanical issue.

Car PDI Guide: What Is PDI, Why Do You Need It and What Does It Check?

Answers to the practical questions buyers ask before booking a car PDI, from what PDI means to where to get an independent inspection and what a report should contain.

What is car PDI?

Car PDI means Pre-Delivery Inspection. In practical terms, it is a structured inspection of a vehicle before a buyer accepts delivery. For a new car, the purpose is to document observable condition before handover. For a used car, the appropriate service is usually a used-car inspection or pre-purchase inspection before buying.

Why is car PDI important?

A vehicle can have visible issues, transit marks, paint variation, feature problems, missing items or other observations that are easier to address before handover or purchase. An independent inspection gives the buyer documented observations and evidence to support the decision. It cannot guarantee that every hidden or future defect will be detected.

What does a professional car PDI check?

The exact scope depends on the vehicle and service, but a structured inspection can cover exterior and paint condition, panel alignment, tyres and wheels, interior, electrical features, warning indicators, vehicle identity and relevant delivery items. What should a professional car PDI checklist cover?

A useful checklist should be practical enough for a buyer to understand what was actually reviewed. Depending on the vehicle and access, that can include:

  • Vehicle identity: VIN/chassis details and agreed delivery information.
  • Exterior: paint, panels, panel alignment, glass, lamps and visible damage or repair clues.
  • Wheels and tyres: visible condition, tyre markings and wheel damage.
  • Interior: seats, trim, controls, AC, infotainment and other accessible features.
  • Electrical and safety features: warning indicators, lights, cameras, sensors and applicable controls.
  • Diagnostics: selected OBD checks where the vehicle, package and tool support them.
  • Evidence: photographs and clear observations so the buyer can discuss findings with the dealer or seller.

The exact checklist should always be matched to the vehicle and service scope rather than assuming that every system can be fully tested.

Should I get an independent PDI or rely only on the dealer?

A dealer may perform its own pre-delivery checks, but an independent inspection gives the buyer a separate assessment and documented findings. The value is not simply the word “independent”; it is the inspection scope, evidence, reporting and clear explanation of limitations.

What should I do if the inspection finds a defect?

Document the finding, review the evidence and discuss the issue with the dealer or seller before completing the handover or purchase decision. An inspection helps you make a better-informed decision; it does not negotiate or guarantee a particular outcome.

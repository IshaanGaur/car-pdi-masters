EV Car PDI Guide: Why Is It Important and What Should You Check?

A practical guide to electric-car delivery inspection, including charging-related items, displays, controls, electrical features, tyres, safety observations and the limits of an inspection.

What is EV Car PDI?

EV Car PDI is a pre-delivery inspection adapted to an electric vehicle. It checks the vehicle's observable condition and relevant EV-specific delivery items before the buyer accepts handover.

Why is EV PDI important?

EVs combine normal vehicle components with charging, electrical and software-controlled features. A structured inspection can help document visible condition, controls, displays, charging-related items and other applicable features before delivery.

What does EV Car PDI include?

Depending on the vehicle and inspection package, the scope can include exterior and paint, tyres and wheels, interior, displays and controls, electrical features, charging-related items, warning indicators and photo-supported evidence. The exact diagnostic capability depends on the vehicle and supported tools.

What should I check before taking EV delivery?

Review exterior condition, paint and panel alignment, tyres and wheels, charging equipment supplied with the vehicle, charging-port condition, displays and controls, lights, cameras, sensors and warning indicators, along with VIN and agreed delivery items.

EV PDI checklist before delivery

For an electric car, the delivery inspection should combine the normal new-car checks with EV-specific items that are actually accessible:

  • Charging port: visible condition, fit and obvious damage.
  • Charging equipment: confirm the supplied cable, charger or accessories against the agreed delivery list.
  • Displays and warnings: instrument cluster messages, charging status and visible warning indicators.
  • Electrical features: applicable lights, controls, cameras, sensors, infotainment and comfort features.
  • Exterior and running gear: paint, panel alignment, glass, wheels and tyres.
  • Vehicle identity: VIN/chassis and agreed variant or delivery details.

Do not treat a normal PDI as a complete high-voltage battery certification. Diagnostic depth depends on the vehicle, supported systems, tools and appointment conditions.

What should an EV buyer verify beyond the charging port?

The charging port is only one part of an EV delivery check. A buyer should also verify the items that can be observed without treating the inspection as a high-voltage system certification:

  • Charging equipment: match the supplied cable, charger and accessories with the agreed delivery list.
  • Instrument display: look for visible warning messages, unusual alerts and charging-related indicators.
  • Range information: record the displayed information as an observation rather than treating it as a laboratory measurement of battery capacity.
  • Controls and software-driven features: operate applicable controls, infotainment, cameras and sensors within the inspection scope.
  • Tyres and wheels: check visible condition and any damage before handover.
  • Vehicle identity: verify VIN/chassis and the agreed variant or delivery details.

This approach gives the buyer useful evidence while keeping the inspection's limits clear.

What EV-specific issues should make a buyer pause?

A visible warning message, charging-equipment mismatch, damaged charging port, unexplained physical damage or a feature that does not behave as expected should be documented and raised with the dealer before handover. One observation does not automatically establish the cause of a fault; the useful next step is to have the dealer explain and rectify the issue, then record the outcome.

Does EV PDI check battery health?

Do not assume that a visual or standard inspection provides a complete battery-health assessment. Battery diagnostics depend on the vehicle, supported systems and available tools. Car PDI Masters describes EV inspection around the items actually supported by the inspection process rather than promising a universal battery-health guarantee.

What should I carry or confirm before an EV delivery inspection?

Before the appointment, confirm the exact vehicle variant, VIN/chassis information when available, the agreed accessories and charging equipment, and whether the inspection location provides the access needed for the agreed scope. If a particular diagnostic or charging test matters to the buyer, it is better to confirm its availability in advance than assume every EV system can be tested with every tool.

Does EV PDI include charging checks?

Charging-related items can be checked where accessible and within the agreed inspection scope. The exact test depends on the vehicle, charging equipment and appointment conditions.

What if an EV shows a warning or feature issue?

Document the warning or feature behaviour, photograph relevant evidence where useful and raise the finding with the dealer before accepting delivery. An inspection records observations; it does not guarantee that every software, battery or future issue will be detected.

Is EV PDI different from normal New Car PDI?

The core inspection principles are similar, but an EV has additional charging and electrical considerations. The inspection scope should therefore be matched to the vehicle and the tools that can actually support it.

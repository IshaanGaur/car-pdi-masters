Used Car PDI & Inspection Guide: What Should You Check Before Buying?

Practical answers for buyers asking what a used car inspection checks, how paint and accident observations are assessed, what documents matter and when to inspect.

What is used car PDI?

For a used vehicle, the goal is a structured pre-purchase inspection of the vehicle's observable condition before the buyer commits. It can be called used car PDI, used car inspection or pre-purchase inspection depending on the buying situation.

Why is used car inspection important?

A used vehicle has a history that may not be obvious from a short test drive. A structured inspection can document observable condition and help a buyer understand issues before negotiating or purchasing.

What does used car inspection include?

Scope can include body and paint, panel alignment, glass and lamps, tyres and wheels, interior, electrical features, accessible mechanical areas, warning indicators and relevant documents or vehicle identity observations. The exact scope depends on access and the vehicle.

What should I check on a used car before buying?

A used-car inspection should focus on the areas that can materially change the buying decision. Depending on access and scope, that includes:

  • Identity and history clues: VIN/chassis details and available service or vehicle records.
  • Body and paint: panel alignment, paint-thickness differences and visible repair or accident-related clues.
  • Flood or water-damage clues: unusual moisture, corrosion, odour or other observable signs that warrant further investigation.
  • Tyres, wheels, brakes and suspension: visible condition and symptoms that can be observed within the inspection scope.
  • Interior and electricals: controls, AC, infotainment, lights, cameras, sensors and warning indicators.
  • Mechanical observations: accessible engine-bay areas, leaks, noises or other observable symptoms within the agreed scope.
  • Diagnostics: selected OBD scanning on supported vehicles, interpreted together with physical findings.

No checklist can reconstruct a vehicle's entire past or guarantee future mechanical reliability. The value is in documenting evidence before you commit to the purchase.

Can a paint thickness gauge identify an accident car?

Paint thickness measurements can identify coating differences between applicable panels and can support the inspectioner's observations. They are useful evidence, but thickness readings alone cannot prove the full accident history of a vehicle.

Can inspection check the engine and gearbox?

An inspection can document accessible and observable mechanical conditions and relevant symptoms within its defined scope. It should not be described as a guarantee of internal engine or transmission condition unless those components have actually been tested to that extent.

Does used car inspection include OBD scanning?

Selected OBD scanning may be performed on supported vehicles and packages. Results are limited to compatible systems and should be interpreted together with physical inspection findings.

When should I inspect a used car?

Ideally, inspect before you commit to the purchase or pay a non-refundable amount. Confirm that the vehicle can be accessed at the seller, dealer or agreed location and that the seller permits an independent inspection.

Can an inspection help me negotiate the price?

It can provide documented observations that a buyer can use during a price discussion. The final negotiation depends on the buyer, seller and market value.

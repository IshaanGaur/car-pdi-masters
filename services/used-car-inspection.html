BEFORE YOU BOOK

Prepare for a Used Car Inspection

A little preparation helps the inspection focus on the vehicle and the questions that matter to you. Coordinate access with the seller before confirming the appointment.

Share the Vehicle Details

Keep the make, model, variant, registration year and the seller’s stated odometer reading handy. Share the inspection location and the best contact number for appointment coordination.

Ask the Seller for Available Records

Request any available registration, insurance, service invoices, repair bills and previous inspection records. These are seller-provided documents for your own review; the physical inspection does not authenticate their legal validity or establish a complete vehicle history.

Arrange Access to the Car

Confirm that the seller has agreed to the inspection and that the vehicle will be accessible at the agreed place and time. Mention any known issue or specific concern when booking.

Buyer reminder: Try to complete the inspection before making the final purchase decision or payment. Review the findings alongside the seller’s disclosures and independently verify ownership, finance, insurance and other records through the appropriate official channels.

SECOND-HAND CAR BUYER GUIDE

What a Used Car Inspection Can—and Cannot—Tell You

A pre-purchase used car inspection helps you review the vehicle’s observable condition before you make a decision. It is designed to document findings from the checks that can be carried out at the agreed inspection location and time.

Condition you can review

Recorded observations can help you discuss visible body and paint condition, tyre wear, interior condition, accessible features and other inspected areas with the seller.

Questions to take to the seller

Use relevant findings to ask about repair history, warning indicators, replaced panels, maintenance records or any condition that needs clarification. Ask for supporting records where available.

Checks that need separate verification

A physical inspection does not authenticate ownership or documents, confirm finance or challan status, establish complete accident history, or replace a specialist mechanical diagnosis or road test where those are not part of the agreed scope.

Buying tip: Share the make, model, variant, registration year and seller location when enquiring. If you are arranging an inspection before deciding whether to buy, see our pre-purchase car inspection service for the buyer-focused process.

UNDERSTANDING THE FINDINGS

How to Use Your Used Car Inspection Report

The report is a record of the checks and observations made during the inspection. Use it as one input to your buying decision, not as a substitute for document verification or your own assessment of the seller and vehicle.

Review the Checkpoint Result

Read each recorded result with its remarks. Pay attention to observations that may need repair estimates, clarification from the seller or a closer review by a qualified workshop.

Match Photos to the Remarks

Where photographs are included, use them to understand the specific visible condition recorded. Not every checkpoint requires a photograph, so read the written result as well.

Ask Follow-up Questions

Discuss unclear findings with the seller. If an observation needs specialist diagnosis, arrange an appropriate follow-up check before deciding whether to proceed.

Inspection limitation: A physical inspection reflects observable condition and checks within the defined workflow at that time. It cannot guarantee that a vehicle is free of every defect, reveal every concealed issue or independently confirm its complete accident, repair or service history.

USED CAR INSPECTION FAQS

Common Questions About Used Car Inspection

If you are comparing services, our PDI for old car service is the handover-focused option for a used vehicle, while this page focuses on the broader buyer inspection process.

When should I book a used car inspection?

The inspection should ideally be completed before final purchase or handover where possible, so relevant observations can be reviewed before the transaction is completed.

What does a used car inspection check?

The inspection covers relevant visible areas such as body and paint, glass and lights, tyres and wheels, interior controls, electrical features, accessible engine-bay areas, relevant mechanical observations and safety-related items.

What is an old car inspection?

An old car inspection is a structured check of a second-hand vehicle's observable condition before purchase. It can help document relevant findings across the inspected areas, but it does not replace document verification or specialist diagnosis.

Can a used car inspection confirm the complete vehicle history?

No. The inspection records observations from the vehicle and checks included in the defined workflow. It does not by itself guarantee a complete historical record of every accident, repair or service event.

Does the inspection verify ownership, loan status or pending challans?

No. A physical vehicle inspection is not a legal, ownership, finance, insurance or challan verification service. Review the relevant documents and verify records independently through the appropriate official channels before purchase.

What should I keep ready before booking?

Keep the car’s make, model, variant, registration year, seller contact and inspection location handy. Ask the seller to make available any service invoices or other vehicle records they have, and confirm access to the car for the appointment.

Do you provide an inspection report?

The inspection workflow records checkpoint results, observations and relevant photo evidence in the inspection report.

Does every checkpoint require a photograph?

No. Photo requirements depend on the inspection workflow and the condition or result of the individual checkpoint.

Does the inspection guarantee that a used car has no defects?

No inspection can guarantee that a vehicle has no defects. The report reflects observations and checks performed within the defined inspection scope at the time of inspection.
